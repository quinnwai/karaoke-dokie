# karaoke-dokie

Find karaoke songs that fit your voice, from your own Spotify saved songs.

You hold a button and sing your lowest note, then your highest. The app signs you in to Spotify,
looks up the vocal range of each saved song, and lists the songs that fit your range, labeled
Easy, Medium or Hard. Songs whose range is only an estimate come after them, under Maybe. Phone
first, as a website.

## Features

Each feature lists where it lives and how it is tested. Run every test with `npm test` (unit and
integration, Vitest) and `npm run test:e2e` (end to end, Playwright).

| Feature | What it does | Code | Tests |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Note detection | Finds the pitch of each 46 ms audio frame (YIN), then the note held longest in a press-and-hold recording, ignoring breaths, slides and vibrato. | `src/lib/audio/` | `tests/audio/`: synthetic tones E2 to C6, voice-like tones with vibrato and noise, and 20 labeled sung notes from NSynth |
| Range test screen | Landing, "find a quiet spot", hold-to-record lowest and highest note with a live pitch dot, the detected note name, a wordless bouncing arrow (bottom right) to continue. | `src/components/`, `src/lib/client/` | `tests/screen/` (flow reducer, components), `tests/e2e/screen.spec.ts` (fake mic plays A2 and C5) |
| Pick songs you sing well | Instead of the test, choose up to 3 known songs; their lowest and highest notes become your range. | `src/components/PickSongs.tsx` | `tests/screen/`, `tests/e2e/screen.spec.ts` |
| Spotify sign-in | Authorization code flow; reads the whole library once (every liked song, three top lists, the last 50 plays, own playlists), keeps it per user in Postgres and then syncs only what changed (at most 5 requests on a return visit). Real Spotify only on a deployed preview or production or with `SPOTIFY_LIVE=1`; scripts use the mock or a snapshot unless `--live`. A 429 that says `QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, or waits over 30 s, is kept as a block (`spotify_block`) that the site and the scripts honour: no request until it passes. When the library cannot be read the list is the catalogue's, with `libraryUnavailable` and a quiet note, never a 5xx. See `docs/backend.md`, Spotify rate limit. | `src/lib/server/spotify.ts`, `app/api/auth/` | `tests/server/spotify.test.ts`, `tests/server/spotifyBlock.test.ts`, `tests/server/routes.test.ts` |
| Song ranges from note files | Parses UltraStar note files: lowest and highest note (ignoring extremes that take up under 5% of sung time), tessitura, fast-lyrics flag (at least a tenth of the syllables start a 10 s stretch above 4.5 syllables per second; "~" continuations don't count). Fixes files written in the wrong octave. Duets get each singer's part measured the same way (lines both sing count for each), and are matched and shown on the part that fits you best; duets stored earlier get their parts from re-running `ranges:build` and `ranges:import`, or from `-- --rescan-duets` on `usdb:sync` (library songs) or `usdb:fill` (catalogue songs). | `src/lib/songs/ultrastar.ts`, `range.ts`, `match.ts` | `tests/songs/` |
| Redo one end | Tap either note of your range on the results page to re-sing just that end; "redo" restarts the test. Nothing is flagged or asked: the range is the notes the detector heard, as sung. | `src/components/Results.tsx`, `src/lib/client/flow.ts` | `tests/screen/rangeCheck.test.tsx`, `flow.test.ts`, `app.test.tsx` |
| Matching and labels | Only songs inside your range are shown. Dub, instrumental and karaoke versions are left out (they share the original's name but have little singing); remixes and live versions keep the original's range. Labels come from how much of the singing sits near your edges (the outer eighth of your range at each end): Hard if 10% or more, Medium if 2% or more or if a quarter sits outside the middle half, Easy otherwise. The reason line follows the label. Songs that fit an octave up or down are listed after, with "Sing it higher/lower than the artist". Low-confidence ranges (artist estimates, mostly-rap or thinly sung recordings) get no label: they go in a Maybe group after every other song when they fit your range give or take 2 semitones (as written or an octave away). | `src/lib/songs/match.ts`, `rank.ts` | `tests/songs/match.test.ts`, `rank.test.ts`, `tests/screen/maybe.test.tsx` |
| Ranking | Songs you know (Spotify top tracks over about 4 weeks, 6 months and a year, recent plays, or your own playlists) come first; saved-only songs follow. Within each group every song gets one score: 85% comfort × familiarity (comfort is the share of the song's sung time in the middle of your range), plus 15% popularity (Spotify popularity, USDB views, karaoke views, Billboard Year-End charts, all on one scale). Octave shifts and artist estimates lose 0.10, fast lyrics 0.05. The whole library is ranked, then paged; each song shows its top one or two reasons. | `src/lib/songs/score.ts`, `rank.ts`, `src/lib/server/spotify.ts` | `tests/songs/score.test.ts`, `tests/server/spotify.test.ts`, `tests/server/songService.test.ts` |
| Setlist ranking | The engine behind the Setlist tuner (QWW-47, the round 5 design's distance ranking): the songs that fit your range, each given a place from 0 to 100 on Difficulty, Listening history, Popularity and Tempo by its rank among them, then sorted by the weighted distance to the tuner's gear values (Popularity counts double), with Era as an OR filter and any gear reset to leave it out. The defaults lean on popularity, then easiest; easy leans toward the lower-middle of your range; a song we do not know on an active gear comes after the rest; a one-note range has no difficulty. `POST /api/setlist`, with your library added when you are signed in. See docs/backend.md, Setlist ranking. | `src/lib/songs/setlist.ts`, `setlistConfig.ts`, `src/lib/server/setlistService.ts`, `app/api/setlist/` | `tests/songs/setlist.test.ts`, `tests/server/setlist.test.ts` |
| Karaoke popularity | Spotify no longer sends popularity, so songs also get it from our own karaoke data: the YouTube views of each song's KaraFun karaoke videos, averaged with Spotify and USDB views, and a floor for Billboard Year-End Hot 100 songs (0.62, or 0.73 for the top 20, a "Crowd favourite"). Every source is on one scale: a song's share of catalogue songs with fewer USDB views, with karaoke views and Spotify linked to it on the songs that have both, so a typical song scores the same whichever source we have (`src/lib/popularity/calibrate.ts`). USDB stars are left out, since nearly every song has 5. `npm run popularity:calibrate` rebuilds the scale from the database into `src/lib/songs/popularityScale.ts`; rerun it after `usdb:index` or `popularity:import` changes the numbers a lot. The whole list and the catalogue use it, and a song with no USDB or Spotify year shows its Year-End chart year. After merging, load it with `npm run popularity:import -- --karafun karafun_songs.csv --billboard billboard_songs.csv` (needs `DATABASE_URL`; `--dry-run` only prints the summary). Both files come from the vocal-range pipeline and stay out of this repo. | `src/lib/popularity/`, `scripts/popularity-import.ts`, `scripts/popularity-calibrate.ts`, `src/lib/songs/score.ts`, `popularityScale.ts` | `tests/popularity/`, `tests/songs/score.test.ts`, `tests/server/` |
| Tempo | The Tempo slider (Slow jams to Upbeat tracks) sorts by beats per minute. Offline (`npm run tempo:fill`, `tempo:ultrastar`), never at request time: Deezer's `bpm`, else a beat tracker on the 30 s Deezer preview (audio deleted after), else the note file's `#BPM`; each folded into 70 to 180. See docs/backend.md. |
| Artist fallback | No range of its own: use the typical range of the artist's other songs that have a note file or a measured recording (never another estimate; one recording counts once), marked low confidence. Nothing at all: the song is hidden and logged. | `src/lib/songs/artistFallback.ts`, `resolve.ts` | `tests/songs/`, `tests/server/wiring.test.ts` |
| USDB download | Logs in to USDB, finds the song, downloads its note file, stores only the computed numbers. Requests are 2.5 s apart, and each download waits out the site's own countdown (about 25 s per song). Also stores USDB views and stars; `-- --rescan` re-downloads older songs to add the pitch histogram. Run with `npm run usdb:sync`. | `src/lib/usdb/`, `scripts/usdb-sync.ts` | `tests/usdb/` (fake pages) |
| USDB song index | Keeps our own copy of USDB's song list (id, artist, title, views, stars, genre, year, language, last change; no note files). The first run reads all of it, 100 rows per page (about 14 minutes); later runs read only what changed, and `-- --full` reads all of it again. When it's filled, the sync looks songs up there instead of searching, so a song USDB doesn't have costs no request. Same approach as USDB Syncer. Run with `npm run usdb:index`. | `src/lib/usdb/songIndex.ts`, `scripts/usdb-index.ts` | `tests/usdb/songIndex.test.ts`, `tests/usdb/sync.test.ts` |
| USDB catalogue fill | Computes ranges for every indexed USDB song that has none yet, most-viewed first, one song per key. Waits out the countdown unless `--skip-countdown` (like USDB Syncer; OK'd by the USDB folks). Stop and rerun any time. Run with `npm run usdb:fill`. | `src/lib/usdb/fillCatalogue.ts`, `scripts/usdb-fill.ts` | `tests/usdb/fillCatalogue.test.ts` |
| Coverage report | A CSV of one person's whole library: range source (note file, artist estimate, none), song range, whether it fits their voice, rank in the app, and for songs without a note file whether USDB lacks it or has it under a slightly different name. Read-only. Run with `npm run report:coverage -- --out report.csv`. | `src/lib/report/coverage.ts`, `scripts/coverage-report.ts` | `tests/report/coverage.test.ts` |
| Density report | For one person's library, how many matched songs fall in each decade, label (Easy, Medium, Hard), with and without known popularity, and duets, for the main list and Maybe apart: what the results screen's filters would have behind them. Uses the app's own whole-list results. Read-only, except that an expired Spotify sign-in is refreshed and saved, as the app does. Run with `npm run report:facets`. | `src/lib/report/facets.ts`, `readOnlyStore.ts`, `scripts/facets-report.ts` | `tests/report/facets.test.ts`, `readOnlyStore.test.ts` |
| Credits | `/credits` lists every source, linked from the bottom of the results. A song's info button links to its USDB page when we know it. | `src/lib/credits.ts`, `app/credits/` | `tests/screen/icons.test.tsx` |
| Bulk note ranges | Reads folders of UltraStar note files from public collections, computes each song's range, drops implausible ones, and writes only the numbers to a JSON file (`npm run ranges:build -- --out ranges.json name=folder ...`). `npm run ranges:import -- ranges.json` loads it into the database without overwriting songs that already have a note-file range. | `src/lib/notes/bulkImport.ts`, `scripts/note-ranges-*.ts` | `tests/notes/bulkImport.test.ts` |
| Ranges from recordings | The vocal-range pipeline measures full recordings the app's way (the same 5th to 95th percentile of sung time) and exports `karaoke_ranges.json`, which `npm run ranges:import` loads with each row's own info line. Sources rank note file > recording > artist estimate > nothing: a better source replaces a worse one, a newer export (a new `method`) replaces an older one's rows, and nothing replaces a note file. The USDB sync and fill still look recorded songs up, so a note file can replace them. Quinn-Wai cleared the first batch, 3,095 Billboard Year-End songs, to be shown (Oct 18; the pipeline's `docs/09_data_rights.md`, first section); each later batch is Quinn-Wai's call. | `src/lib/songs/sourceRank.ts`, `src/lib/notes/bulkImport.ts`, `src/lib/usdb/` | `tests/notes/bulkImport.test.ts`, `tests/usdb/` |
| Results and song detail | The phone gets every match at once and orders, filters and pages it itself, 5 songs at a time with "Generate more". **Tune** (pink): sliders for preferences, each untouched until moved: Ease in to Whole range (how much of your range a song uses, and how little of it sits in your comfortable middle), Deep cuts to On repeat (how much you play it) and Hidden gems to Big hits (popularity, offered only when most songs have real popularity data). The songs closest to every set slider come first, so a slider reorders and never removes a song; a song sits on a slider by its rank in the list, not by its raw score, so every tenth of the track holds about a tenth of the songs and the list changes along the whole length (songs with the same score are told apart by Spotify's order); the lyric line says what the slider moved last does, and Reset unsets them all. Songs you know stay above saved-only ones, with a "Saved, not played lately" divider, until you set On repeat yourself; Maybe songs stay last. **Pick** (cyan): decade chips, plus mood and duet chips only while `DUETS_AND_MOODS=on` (off by default: the API then sends no mood labels and no duet markers, so every duet and mood control disappears, and the order of the songs is the same either way; `src/lib/server/features.ts`), one pick per row, each showing how many songs it holds given the other picks; a chip with none is disabled, so no tap empties the list. Each row shows the year, the label, octave and fast-lyrics icons, and one or two reasons, led by the one that matches the slider moved last; a duet says whose part to take (switch on). Detail shows the song's range over yours (a duet shows your part), year and genre, the label, moods (switch on), an info button saying where the data came from, and Spotify's own player (plays in the page; its Spotify logo goes to the song on Spotify, which tries the Spotify app first). A song with no track to embed (catalogue songs, and the few offline-mock songs without a real id) gets a plain "Open in Spotify" ("Find on Spotify" for catalogue songs) link instead. The player is Spotify's page in an iframe, so Spotify sets its own cookies; the credits page says so. Maybe songs show without a label, and their info button says the range is an estimate. | `src/components/Results.tsx`, `SongDetail.tsx`, `SpotifyPlayer.tsx`, `src/lib/client/spotifyEmbed.ts`, `src/lib/songs/browse.ts`, `src/lib/client/flow.ts` | `tests/songs/browse.test.ts` (including a seeded property test that no tap empties the list), `tests/songs/sliderSweep.test.ts` (each slider swept in 10% steps on catalogue and library lists shaped like the real ones), `tests/screen/`, `tests/e2e/` |
| No Spotify (catalogue mode) | After the range test, "Continue" shows songs from our catalogue straight away, with no sign-in: every song with a note-file or recording range (never an artist estimate, which would only exist because of someone's library), most popular first, matched against the range sent with the request. "Connect Spotify" (invite-only during the beta) carries the range over to an account and switches to the library. On repeat needs listening data, so catalogue mode has no On repeat slider. | Beta event log | What each tester does, keyed by a random device id and never a Spotify id or a song from someone's library: the onboarding funnel (landing, start, each note, range shown, results), slider moves and chip taps, songs opened with their place in the list, taps on the plain Spotify link (not plays in Spotify's player) and thumbs. `GET /api/admin/events` shows the funnel, a tally per control in use and each device's session in order. Off unless `EVENTS=on`, or in production with Postgres. | `src/lib/client/funnel.ts`, `src/lib/client/events.ts`, `app/api/events/`, `src/lib/server/eventStats.ts` | `tests/screen/funnel.test.ts`, `tests/screen/events.test.ts`, `tests/server/events.test.ts`, `tests/server/eventStats.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/fullstack.spec.ts` |
| Feedback | One-tap "How'd it go?" thumbs up or down, stored with your range, the label (or Maybe), the octave note, and where the song's range came from at that moment (source, method, lowest and highest note), so later imports cannot change what a tap was about, plus the results view (sliders and chips) the song was found in. | Database | Saved ranges, song ranges, the data-source log and feedback. Postgres (Neon) in production, memory store locally. Every write is read back in one shared test suite run against both. | `src/lib/server/*Store.ts`, `db/schema.sql` | `tests/server/storeContract.test.ts` (pg-mem) |
| Admin log | Hidden page listing every song's data source or skip reason. Needs the `x-admin-token` header. | `app/api/admin/log/` | `tests/server/routes.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/fullstack.spec.ts` |
| Mood labels | Offline, never at request time. Songs are labelled Upbeat and/or Heartfelt by AcousticBrainz's audio analysis (CC0, by MusicBrainz recording ID; `npm run moods:acousticbrainz`) and by an LLM from artist, title and year, which may say it doesn't know the song (`npm run moods:label -- --provider openai`, resumable; also `gemini` or `anthropic`). A mood may become a chip only if it passes the precision gate against Quinn-Wai's hand-labelled gold set (`npm run moods:report`), and reaches the screen only when `DUETS_AND_MOODS=on` and `MOODS_SHIPPED` lists it (e.g. `MOODS_SHIPPED=upbeat,heartfelt`). See [data/moods/README.md](data/moods/README.md). | `src/lib/moods/`, `scripts/moods-*.ts` | `tests/moods/`, `tests/server/storeContract.test.ts` |

## Run it

```sh
npm install
cp .env.example .env.local   # fill in, or use the offline demo below
npm run dev
```

Offline demo, no accounts needed: `KARAOKE_STORE=memory SPOTIFY_MOCK=1 SESSION_SECRET=<32+ chars> npm run dev`.
Mock mode signs you in as "Test Singer" with 25 well-known songs, and seeds the memory store with
39 placeholder songs from general knowledge (`data/seed-song-ranges.json`): 24 from that library
(five stand in for estimates and show under Maybe), plus 15 catalogue-only songs so the no-Spotify
mode has every decade and three duets. Their years and genres are real; their ranges, USDB views and
moods are placeholders. Duets and moods are hidden unless `DUETS_AND_MOODS=on` (QWW-43): add that and
`MOODS_SHIPPED=upbeat,heartfelt` to see the Duets chip, the "Duet: take ..." lines and the mood chips, and
`EVENTS=on` to try the event log. Real ranges come from USDB and the vocal-range pipeline.

End-to-end tests build and start the app in mock mode. In the cloud sandbox, point Playwright at
the preinstalled Chromium: `PW_CHROMIUM_PATH=/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194/chrome-linux/chrome npm run test:e2e`.
Screenshots of every step land in `test-results/screens/`.
